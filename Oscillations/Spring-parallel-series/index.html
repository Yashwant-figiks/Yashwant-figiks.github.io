Physics Formulas

Hooke's Law: The force exerted by a spring is proportional to how much it's stretched or compressed.

F = k · x x = F / k

Series Springs: Springs connected end-to-end. The same force passes through each spring, but the total extension is the sum of individual extensions.

1/k_eq = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂ k_eq = (k₁ · k₂) / (k₁ + k₂) x_total = mg / k_eq = x₁ + x₂ x₁ = mg / k₁ , x₂ = mg / k₂

Parallel Springs: Springs connected side-by-side. Both stretch by the same amount, and forces add up.

k_eq = k₁ + k₂ x = mg / k_eq F₁ = k₁ · x , F₂ = k₂ · x F₁ + F₂ = mg

💡 Key insight: In series, the softer spring dominates the stretch. In parallel, stiffer springs carry more load. Parallel arrangement always gives a higher equivalent stiffness (less stretch) than series.
