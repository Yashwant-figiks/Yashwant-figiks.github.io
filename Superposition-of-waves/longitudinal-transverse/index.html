Case 2: Longitudinal Wave
Transverse wave: The particles of the medium move perpendicular to the direction the wave travels. Imagine flicking a rope — the bump moves horizontally while each point on the rope moves up and down.
Studying reflection: Click Add Wall to place a fixed boundary. A never-ending wave keeps arriving and would blend with what bounces back, so switch to 1 Pulse or 2 Pulses first — that sends just a short wave train so you can clearly watch it travel out, hit the wall, invert, and return.
Longitudinal wave: The particles of the medium move parallel to the direction the wave travels. Think of a Slinky pushed back and forth — particles bunch closer together (compression) and spread further apart (rarefaction) as the disturbance travels along the same axis.
Studying reflection: Click Add Wall to place a fixed boundary. Switch to 1 Pulse or 2 Pulses so only a short burst of compressions is sent — otherwise the continuous stream from the source would keep mixing with the echo bouncing back off the wall.
